As an organisation owner, you can give a researcher a new project in two ways:

| Route | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| [Create the project directly](#create-a-project-directly) | A researcher asks you for one. |
| [Share the group invitation link](#share-a-group-invitation-link) | Researchers set up their own projects. |

Either way, the researcher becomes the project's project manager.
The new project has no allocation yet.
The project manager orders one, and the order comes to you.
See [Approve an order](approve-requests.md#approve-an-order).

For the researcher's side, see [Requesting a new project](../project-managers/request-new-project.md).

!!! note "Renewals are not new projects"
    To keep an existing project going, its project manager extends the allocation's end date.
    Do not create a new project for a renewal. A new project gets a new project code.
    See [Extending a request](../project-managers/extend-request.md).

## Create a project directly

Ask the researcher for:

- a name for the project;
- a short description of the research;
- the email address they use to log in to the portal.

Then create the project:

1. Open your organisation and click the **Projects** tab.
2. Click **Add**.
3. In the **Create project** dialog, enter the **Project name** and **Project description**.
   Use a name that no other project in your organisation has.
4. Choose an **OECD FoS code** if you know it.
5. Click **Create**.
   The portal shows **Project has been created.** and opens the project.

Then add the researcher as project manager:

1. On the project, click the **Team** tab.
2. Click **Add**, then **Invite by mail**.
3. Enter the researcher's **Email** and choose **Project manager** as the **Role**.
4. Click **Continue**, then **Send invitation**.

The researcher gets an email with a link to accept.
If the researcher already belongs to a project in your organisation,
you can click **Add** → **Member** instead, find them under **User**,
choose the role and click **Add role**.
The portal does not email them in that case, so let them know.

The project gets its project code automatically,
usually within an hour.

## Share a group invitation link

REANNZ creates one link for your organisation.
Researchers who open it can request a project in your organisation.
A link is either approved automatically or needs your approval.

To find the link:

1. Open your organisation and click **Team**.
2. Click the **Group invitations** tab.
3. Expand the invitation.
   **Auto approve** shows whether the link needs your approval.
4. Copy the **Invitation link** with the copy icon next to it.

Requests through a link that needs approval wait for you.
See [Approve a project request](../project-managers/request-new-project.md#approve-a-project-request).

You cannot change or cancel the link yourself.
If your organisation has no link, or you want it changed or cancelled,
[Contact our Support Team](mailto:support@nesi.org.nz?subject=HPC%20Portal%20Request).

## Keep the invitation link private

Treat the link as a secret.

- Send it only to researchers at your institution.
- Do not post it on a public web page or share it outside your institution.
- With a link that is approved automatically,
  anyone who logs in with it can create a project in your organisation.
  The portal does not email you when this happens.
- Typing the name of an existing project joins that project as project manager.
  Check **User permission requests** under **Group invitations** to see who has used the link.

If you think the link has leaked,
[contact our Support Team](mailto:support@nesi.org.nz?subject=HPC%20Portal%20invitation%20link) to have it replaced.
